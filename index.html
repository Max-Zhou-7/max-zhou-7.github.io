I’m Zilu Zhou, and I go by Max. I’m a master’s student in Computer Science at Northeastern University’s Khoury College, graduating in December 2026, and a student researcher in Prof. Ehsan Elhamifar’s MCADS lab. I work on egocentric computer vision: understanding video recorded from wearable cameras such as smart glasses. Before Northeastern, I studied applied mathematics at UC Santa Barbara.

I’m looking for research assistant and PhD positions in computer vision. You can reach me at ziluzhou7@gmail.com.

Research interests

  • Multi-person egocentric video. Understanding cooperative activity from the first-person views of the people involved, including reading a partner’s intent from gestures and other visible cues.
  • World models for anticipation. JEPA-style models that predict in latent space, for anticipating what comes next in egocentric video and for detecting errors in procedural tasks.
  • Hands. Telling whose hand is whose, and left from right, when two people work close together and mostly their hands are in view.
  • Toward AR assistance. In the long run, I want perception that is fast and reliable enough to drive real-time AR assistants, starting from the perception side and moving toward interaction.

Current projects

  • Predicting what a partner intends before they say it, from muted first-person video of both people, and testing which visible cues such as gestures make that possible (CS 7170, Multimodal Video Understanding, with Angshuman Basu).
  • Action-conditioned latent forecasting to fill in dropped frames of an egocentric video stream with a JEPA world model (CS 5330, Pattern Recognition and Computer Vision).

In the MCADS lab, I also help collect two-person egocentric video for a project on hand ownership: I set up and synchronized two Project Aria glasses, designed the cooperative tasks, recorded sessions, and tested hand segmentation.
