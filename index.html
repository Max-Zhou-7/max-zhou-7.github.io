Research interests

  • Hands in multi-person egocentric video. When two people work side by side, their hands share the frame while arms and bodies are often out of view. I’m interested in telling whose hand is whose, the wearer’s or a partner’s, and which hand it is, left or right. Current vision-language models still struggle with both.
  • Multi-wearer video understanding. Using two synchronized egocentric views of the same cooperative activity, where each camera can see what the other misses, and reading a partner’s intent from gestures and other visible cues.
  • World models for anticipation. JEPA-style models that predict in latent space, for anticipating what comes next in egocentric video and for detecting errors in procedural tasks.
  • Toward AR assistance. In the long run, I want perception that is fast and reliable enough to drive real-time AR assistants, starting from the perception side and moving toward interaction.

Current research

Whose hand is it? A dual-egocentric benchmark for hand ownership

MCADS Lab, Khoury College, advised by Prof. Ehsan Elhamifar. June 2026 – present

Given a hand visible in one person’s egocentric video during a cooperative task, the benchmark asks whether it belongs to the camera wearer or to their partner, in close-up views where body and arm cues are out of frame. Two people wear Meta Project Aria glasses, time-aligned with TICSync and co-registered in a shared coordinate frame with Aria MPS multi-SLAM.

So far I’ve built and validated the two-headset capture setup, designed the cooperative tasks along three axes (physical coupling, coordination structure, and resource contention), collected recordings, tested hand segmentation with SAM 3, and started baseline evaluations of vision-language models. Next, I plan to build a real-time model that predicts ownership and chirality for each hand in this setting.

Status: data collection in progress. We plan an arXiv preprint with baseline results in winter 2026–27.
