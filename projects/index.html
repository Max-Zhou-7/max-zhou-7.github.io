Reading a partner’s intent from what is visible

CS 7170, Multimodal Video Understanding seminar, with Angshuman Basu

Can a model tell what a partner wants before they say it? On two-person cooking sessions from CoMind, the model sees muted first-person video from both people up to the moment just before someone speaks, plus the recipe, and picks the intent their words express from a set of plausible alternatives. What people said gives the labels for free. A frozen video encoder feeds a small transformer that alternates between local and session-level attention, and between within-person and across-person tokens.

We report accuracy by cue type (gesture, speech, or none) and by how far ahead of speech the prediction is made. We compare against blind, prior, zero-shot video-language, and oracle world-state baselines, and test transfer to IndEgo and HoloAssist. So far we have converted CoMind’s transcripts into about 1,800 labelled intents.

Filling dropped frames by forecasting in latent space

CS 5330, Pattern Recognition and Computer Vision. Two-person team

When frames drop out of a single egocentric video stream, an action-conditioned JEPA world model forecasts the missing frames’ latent representations from past frames only.

Drug toxicity prediction (LD50)

March 2026. Python, PyTorch, scikit-learn, RDKit

Regression models for acute toxicity on the LD50 Zhu benchmark from Therapeutics Data Commons, using ECFP4 Morgan fingerprints. Comparing linear, random forest, and deep models under random and scaffold splits showed the generalization gap that random-split benchmarking hides.

Five architectures (DNN, CNN, RNN/LSTM, Transformer, GNN) for drug classification from SMILES. Graph networks reached 85% accuracy, 12% above the DNN baseline under matched tuning and cross-validation.
